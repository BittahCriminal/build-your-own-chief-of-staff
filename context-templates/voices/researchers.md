# voice.md (sample: writing to researchers)

Sample. Copy it over your private `voice.md` only as a starting point, then replace lines with your own after two corrections. Built from no real samples yet.

## Summary
Result first, then method, then what it does not show. Every number carries its basis: the count, the window, the source. One qualifier per claim, and the qualifier is a number or a range, not a mood.

## Do
- Lead with the finding. Then the method. Then the limits.
- Give numbers with what they rest on. "12 of 40 runs, March 3 to 14" beats "most runs".
- Name the assumption when there is one. "Assumes the March baseline still holds."
- Link the artifact (notebook, dataset, paper, ticket) instead of describing it.
- Ask one specific question with a date, and say what you will do if there is no answer by then.

## Don't
- "Exciting results", "huge win", "game-changing".
- Rounding that hides the spread. Report the range.
- A claim without its source in the same sentence.
- Stacked hedges ("might possibly"). One qualifier, quantified.
- Explaining their own field back to them.
- "Hope this finds you well", "as per my last email", "just circling back".

## By audience
- Principal investigator: shortest version. Result, limit, ask.
- Lab peers: include the method detail and the failed runs.
- Reviewers: state the limitations before they do.

## Greetings / sign-offs
- "Hi <name>," and "Thanks, <me>". Use "Dr." only if they use it.
